This study looks at how risk exposure shapes demand for index-based insurance among herding households in rural Mongolia, where extreme winters pose a threat to livestock and the livelihoods of those who depend on it. We draw on district-level data on livestock mortality and insurance uptake, covering the whole country and spanning eleven years. Results indicate that demand for index insurance increases in areas exposed to adverse winter conditions during the insurance sales period, when pastoralists have to decide about buying insurance coverage for next year’s winter. These results are best explained by availability bias, with households adapting their risk perception in response to adverse weather conditions.
